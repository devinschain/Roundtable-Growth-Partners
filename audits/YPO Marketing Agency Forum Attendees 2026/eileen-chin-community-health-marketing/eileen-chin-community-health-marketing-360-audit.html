Primary Finding

Scope & Identity Check

This audit covers Eileen Chin and Community Health Marketing (CHM), a New York and Tampa multicultural health agency. Her LinkedIn profile (pasted by Devin) lists her as President (since Jan 2019) and Managing Director (since Jan 2012) at CHM, with 12 years at Bristol-Myers Squibb before it, and the email echin@chmagency.com sits on the CHM domain. A search also surfaced another Eileen Chin, President & CEO of R.M. Chin & Associates in Chicago (a transportation and infrastructure firm); that is a different person and is excluded. Two items remain open: the profile shows “CHM + 1 more” as current affiliations without naming the second, and the CHM About page returned an error when fetched, so ownership and founding details come from LinkedIn only. Name collisions also exist for “CHM” (for example, Community Health Media is an unrelated company).

Primary Finding

CHM is a woman- and minority-owned agency with unusually specific, outcome-led proof in a narrow niche: community-based engagement for pharma brands and clinical trials among Black, Hispanic, Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese patients. Its five case studies report 6:1 ROI and 5–30% first-year sales growth (hepatitis), doubled trial enrollment with the patient requirement met in 8 months (immunotherapy), doubled market share in year one (EGFR lung cancer TKI), 20:1 to 8:1 ROI (osteoporosis) and a 110% year (diabetes), and the site lists eleven Digital Television/DTC awards across nine years from 2014 to 2023. Eileen Chin brings 12 years in brand and sales roles at Bristol-Myers Squibb, including hepatitis and cardiovascular specialty sales, which matches the first case study. The weak points are visibility, not capability: no named clients, no named team, no baselines behind the results, an empty news page, and a policy backdrop where the FDA’s diversity-action-plan requirement is stalled. The constraint is turning a credible niche into a sized, documented and regulator-proof offer.
