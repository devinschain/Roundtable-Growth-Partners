Primary Finding

He is a prolific explainer (podcasts, guest articles, a long company blog) and a quiet networker: 9 LinkedIn followers and no posts. For a conversation, the interesting questions are about integration, financing and leadership: how one operator runs a growing group, and how a Montreal-born agency becomes a North American one.

Scope & Identity Check

This audit covers David Morneau and inBeat Agency. His LinkedIn profile (pasted by Devin) lists him as co-founder and CEO of inBeat Agency, based in Montreal, Quebec, with education at Université de Sherbrooke. The profile is dormant: 9 followers, no posts and no experience description. The email david@inbeat.agency sits on the company domain. Three source conflicts are flagged where they matter: (1) Lavery says inBeat acquired Creative Milkshake, while Digital Agency Network says inBeat joined a Fieldtrip-led network after the acquisition; (2) Clutch and the company list Montreal as headquarters while DesignRush lists New York; (3) the minimum engagement is $25,000+ on one directory and $50,000+ on two others. The inbeat.agency About page was read directly; the Clutch, DesignRush and GTM 80/20 pages are directory or competitor content and are tagged accordingly.

Primary Finding

inBeat has evolved from a micro-influencer SaaS-and-agency hybrid into a creative growth agency inside a multi-agency network. It was founded in 2018, lists three offices (Montreal, New York, Los Angeles), a 16-person named team, Meta, TikTok and Google partner badges, and a 4.9 rating on Clutch (9 reviews) and Google (235 reviews via DesignRush). In April 2026 Lavery announced inBeat’s BDC financing and its acquisition of Creative Milkshake, and the site now positions inBeat inside Fieldtrip alongside Creative Milkshake, Bluethings (SEO) and 9AM (media and measurement). The weaknesses are about clarity, not capability: a “#1 Creative Growth Agency in North America” claim with no source, a minimum budget that varies from $25K to $50K+ by directory, only four numeric case studies, and a CEO whose public profile is effectively dark while the group becomes more complex. The constraint is a single, provable story and a visible leader.
