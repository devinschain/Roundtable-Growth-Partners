Primary Finding

Zach Gobst is a strategy and operations consultant who became a clinical-trial recruitment CEO and, more recently, a podcast host. He trained in finance at Indiana University’s Kelley School (2008), worked in purchasing risk at Cummins and in strategy and operations consulting at Verity, co-founded two small startups, then joined Sanguine (a biosamples company) as Director of Operations before becoming CEO of Leapcure in July 2015. He completed 500 Startups Batch 18 in 2016 and has led the company for eleven years through an acquisition (Honeybee Trials, August 2024). He refers to himself in posts as grateful and mission-driven.

Executive Summary

A strategic assessment of Leapcure — a patient recruitment and engagement company for clinical trials, working through advocacy groups, research sites and sponsors.

Visit leapcure.com ↗
Scope & Identity Check

This audit covers Leapcure (leapcure.com), a patient-engagement and recruitment company for clinical trials that calls itself an “EQuitable Clinical Trials” company. The email zach@leapcure.com and Zachary Gobst’s LinkedIn profile (CEO, July 2015 to present) tie the person to the company. Leapcure is not Leap Therapeutics, Leap Health or LeapXpert, which appeared in searches. Headquarters are unclear: press releases are datelined Redwood City, CA (2024 and 2025) and San Francisco (2019), Caplight and Crunchbase list Los Angeles, and Zach lives in Portland, Oregon. Counts differ between sources and over time (see the Executive Summary). On the first fetch the homepage returned only a noindex robots tag and no body text; the content below comes from a second fetch of leapcure.com/en.

Primary Finding

Leapcure is a decade-old, advocacy-network-led patient recruitment company that is repositioning around equity and storytelling. Its site claims 1.4M+ patients impacted, 2.2K+ research sites assisted, 200+ successful studies, 17K+ advocacy partnerships and 49+ countries, and says it does not profit from any individual’s enrollment decision or use patient data to train AI. It acquired Honeybee Trials in August 2024, launched a podcast in June 2025 (now Impactful Breakthroughs) and put its CEO on the SynBioBeta stage in May 2026. The weak points are proof and consistency: no leader, customer or case study is named on the site, no headquarters is stated, the homepage returned a noindex tag on fetch, and the numbers moved between sources (3,500 health organizations in 2019, 13,000 advocacy groups in 42 countries in a May 2026 conference post, 17K+ and 49+ on the site). The constraint is turning a large advocacy network and a strong CEO voice into named, repeatable evidence for sponsors.
